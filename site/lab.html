When the retailer blocks automated requests

Some retail sites return HTTP 403, use dynamic pages, or show CAPTCHA challenges. You can open their product page normally and use this optional browser bookmarklet to capture a price exposed in the open page's structured data. It may not work on every retail site.

Capture Retail Price

Select a component first. Drag “Capture Retail Price” onto your bookmarks bar, open a product page in that browser, and click the bookmark. A new local tab opens for price review; you'll confirm before saving. No login, CAPTCHA bypass or automatic bulk scraping.

2. Markup rule

Sliding markup on the retailer's listed item price; low-cost parts have higher percentages.

At $50 or less: 20% markup
Between $50 and $1,000: gradually decreases
At $1,000 or more: 5% markup

The website applies markup to each individual component. Assembly, inbound freight, customer shipping, taxes, payment fees and other business costs are not automatically included in this local test.

3. How price checks work

  1. Uses a temporary local Edge/Chrome browsing session to inspect public Bing and Google Shopping search-result cards. Does not request retailer product pages for this mode.
  2. The listing must name a preferred merchant, show a USD price, and match the component model family. Generic parts need exact models for reliable comparisons. Failed lookups show an error rather than invented prices.
  3. When more than one source is checked, the builder uses the lowest recent observed price with an in-stock claim, otherwise the lowest recent price with stock unconfirmed.
  4. Search listings do not verify stock. Price estimates expire after 2 hours; the tool never invents missing prices.

A page price is not proof of stock, delivery, purchase eligibility or exact manufacturer part number. Best Buy is included here for price testing only.
